# Meal day

**Date**: 2026-10-30
**Status**: approved
**Issue**: not filed
**Backlog**: [`idea.md`](../../../idea.md) (Meal Planning)
**Slice**: S1 of
[`meal-planning.md`](../slices/meal-planning.md)

From the daily list, `f` opens one day's foods on their own
screen. The weight log keeps its columns and its cursor. A
line is a food and a gram weight. The four numbers on a food
are per 100 g, and the line stores which food, so editing
that food updates every day that uses it. This slice is grams
only.

No production code until these scenarios exist as failing
tests.

## User stories

### US1 — Open today's foods

As a person keeping a daily log, I want `f` to open today's
foods on their own screen, so that I can log a meal without
moving the weight-log cursor or adding a column.

### US2 — See the day add up

As a person logging a meal, I want to add a food and its
grams and see calorie, protein, carbohydrate, and fat
totals, so that the day adds up from what I entered.

### US3 — Correct a food once

As a person who fixes a food's numbers, I want every day
that uses that food to follow, so that I do not edit each
day by hand.

### US4 — Leave the weight log where it was

As a person on the meal screen, I want `[` and `]` to change
only the meal date, and Esc to return to the same log row,
so that the weight log stays where I left it.

## Decisions

1.  **The meal list is its own screen.** `f` on the daily
    list opens it for today. Today is the calendar day the
    today key already uses, so a test can freeze that clock.
    The selected log row does not choose the date. Today
    need not have a daily log row. The screen is not a
    column on the month sheet and not a field on the day
    form.
2.  **Esc from the idle meal screen returns to the list**
    and leaves the selected log row where it was. Esc does
    not quit.
3.  **`[` and `]` on the idle meal screen move one calendar
    day.** They do not move the list selection and they do
    not change the month sheet. The day before 1 March 1992
    is 29 February 1992. The day before 1 January 1990 is
    31 December 1989. There is no stop at the first or last
    weight-log day.
4.  **`[` and `]` on the daily list do what they do now:
    nothing.** They do not open the meal screen, do not
    move the selected row, and do not change the month.
    `[` and `]` on the month sheet, the monthly chart, and
    the long-term chart keep their current actions.
5.  **On the idle meal screen, `f` does not open another
    screen and does not change the date.** `n`, `m`, `c`,
    `l`, `p`, `P`, and `t` do not leave that screen and do
    not change the date or the lines. They do not write a
    PDF. Idle `q` quits the same way `q` quits from the
    list. `ctrl+c` quits from the meal screen too,
    including while a field is open.
6.  **`f` elsewhere stays what it is today.** On a month
    text cell it still starts an edit whose text is `f`.
    On the workout column it still does not start an edit.
    On either chart it does nothing. On the day form it is
    typed text where the field already accepts text, and
    it is not text on the workout field. None of these
    open the meal screen. `n`, `m`, `c`, `l`, `p`, `P`,
    `q`, and `t` keep their current actions. Enter on the
    list still opens the day form.
7.  **A meal does not write the weight log.** Opening or
    editing a meal date does not insert a daily log row
    and does not change weight, sleep, steps, workout,
    note, the trend the list shows, or either chart. The
    list columns stay date, weight, trend, delta, sleep,
    steps, workout, and note.
8.  **One list for that date is the log.** Nothing stores
    a plan beside it. Adding, changing, or removing a
    line is the log. The screen does not show an
    allowance, the words "no allowance", or remaining
    calories.
9.  **The screen shows the date, the lines, and four
    totals.** The heading is `meals` and the date. The
    date is year-month-day with hyphens, the same way the
    list shows a day. A line shows the food name and the
    gram weight to one decimal. The selected line is
    marked with `>`. The line does not show that line's
    own calorie, protein, carbohydrate, or fat figures.
    The totals use the words `calories`, `protein`,
    `carbohydrate`, and `fat`, each the word, a space,
    and the number.
10. **The four numbers are per 100 g.** A line contributes
    each number times grams divided by 100. Totals add
    the lines. Calories are kilocalories. Shown totals
    and shown grams use one decimal the way a body weight
    is shown, including a halfway value. `1.25` grams
    paints as `1.2`. A total of 0.25 paints as `0.2`.
    An exact zero is `0.0`, never `-0.0`. An empty date
    shows `0.0` for each total. Stored grams and stored
    food numbers are not rounded to one decimal. The
    idle line's grams are. Opening grams again shows the
    stored grams, and accepting that text unchanged
    keeps them. Opening the food edit shows each stored
    number the same way, and accepting that text
    unchanged keeps it.
11. **The line stores the grams and which food.** It does
    not store a copy of the four numbers. Editing the
    food changes every line and every meal date that uses
    it. Gram weights do not change. Two lines may name
    the same food. Lines stay in the order they were
    added.
12. **`display_unit` does not scale these grams or these
    totals.** Food mass is not body weight. Kilograms,
    pounds, and stone on the weight log stay the weight
    log. The meal screen does not relabel grams as
    pounds or stone.
13. **A food can be new.** This slice ships no starter
    catalog. A fresh database shows no food names. A name
    is unique after whitespace at either end is removed.
    The stored name is that trimmed name. The match is
    case-sensitive. Space in the middle stays. A blank
    name is refused. The screen shows `error:` and
    `name: blank`.
14. **Up and down move the selected line and stop at the
    ends.** They do not wrap. `j` and `k` do not move it.
    When a date with lines is shown, the first line
    starts selected. An empty date has no selection.
    `x`, Enter, and `e` do nothing when nothing is
    selected. `a` still works. After a line is saved,
    that new line is selected. `x` removes the selected
    line immediately, with no confirm. The food remains.
    The following line becomes selected, or the new last
    line if the removed line was last, or nothing if the
    date is now empty. Empty totals are `0.0`.
15. **`a` opens an add form on the current meal date.**
    The form replaces the line list until Enter or Esc.
    The heading keeps the date. The first field is the
    name, and it is focused, marked with `>`. Tab moves
    to the next field. Tab on the last field returns to
    the name. If the trimmed name matches a stored food,
    the only other field is grams. If it does not, the
    fields are calories, protein, carbohydrate, and fat,
    each labeled per 100 g, then grams. Changing the name
    and pressing Tab follows the new name. Tab does not
    write. Enter saves only when the fields on screen
    match the trimmed name. If the name has changed
    since the last Tab, Enter refuses, the form stays
    open, nothing is written, and the screen shows
    `error:` and `name: press tab`. After that Tab, a
    known name logs grams against that food and does
    not write the four numbers. A new name inserts the
    food and the line together. Esc cancels,
    writes no line and no new food, and the selection is
    whatever it was before `a`. Labels are `name`,
    `calories per 100 g`, `protein per 100 g`,
    `carbohydrate per 100 g`, `fat per 100 g`, and
    `grams`.
16. **A gram value is a number greater than zero and
    nothing else.** Whitespace at either end is removed
    first. A leading `+` is allowed. One decimal point is
    allowed. Zero and negative are refused. A non-number
    is refused. A slash fraction is refused. A unit token
    is refused, including a trailing `g`, and including
    `kg`, `oz`, `lb`, `st`, `cup`, `fl oz`, `tbsp`,
    `tsp`, and `ml`. Fractional grams greater than zero
    are allowed. The same rules apply when editing grams.
    A refused value leaves the form open, writes nothing,
    and does not replace stored grams. The screen shows
    `error:` and `grams: want a number greater than zero`.
17. **A nutrient uses the gram grammar, and zero is
    allowed.** On a new food and on `e`, whitespace at
    either end is removed. A leading `+` is allowed.
    One decimal point is allowed. A fractional number
    is allowed. Empty after trim is missing, not zero.
    A negative is refused. An exponent, `inf`, `NaN`,
    a slash fraction, and a unit token are refused.
    A missing field or a refused number writes no line
    and no new food, and does not change an existing
    food. On `e`, a blank or a refused number refuses
    the whole edit and does not store zero. The screen
    shows `error:` and the field name, then
    `want a number` or, when the number is negative,
    `want a number that is not negative`.
18. **Enter on the idle meal screen edits the selected
    line's grams.** The name is shown and is not a field.
    Enter accepts. Esc cancels and the old grams stay.
    `e` edits the four numbers of that line's food. The
    name is shown and cannot be changed from this screen.
    The editor shows each stored number, not the idle
    tenth. Enter on that text unchanged keeps it. Enter
    accepts a number that passes Decision 17. Esc
    cancels and the old numbers stay. A blank field
    refuses the whole edit, writes nothing, and does
    not store zero. There is no confirm. The key
    reference and the how-to say that saving changes
    every meal date that uses the food. A refused
    number does not replace the stored numbers. Other
    lines and other dates that use the food follow a
    successful edit. The gram weights do not change.
19. **While a form or an edit is open, `[` and `]` do not
    change the meal date.** `a`, `x`, `e`, and `f` do not
    run their idle actions. In the name field they are
    characters, so `q` is a letter and does not quit, and
    `f` is a letter. In a number field they are
    characters, and Enter then refuses a value that is
    not a number under Decisions 16 and 17. Esc cancels
    the form. Idle `q` is the only `q` that quits.
20. **The help lines name the keys.** The list with rows
    uses:

    ```text
    n new   enter edit   m month   c chart   l long   f meal   t today   q quit
    ```

    The empty list uses:

    ```text
    n new day   m month   l long   f meal   t today   q quit
    ```

    A list that failed to load still offers only `q quit`.
    The idle meal screen uses:

    ```text
    a add   enter grams   e food   x remove   [ ] day   esc list   q quit
    ```

    An add form, a grams edit, and a food edit use:

    ```text
    enter save   esc cancel   tab next field
    ```

21. **Meals live in the same database file as the daily
    log,** in new tables, not in new columns. An existing
    database still opens. Its daily log rows are
    unchanged. The file stays mode `0600`. A meal does
    not create a second database file. Quit and open that
    same file again, and `f` shows the same lines for
    that date, in the same order. Another date stays
    empty until it has lines.
22. **This screen does not turn on the alternate screen
    or the mouse.** The program still starts from the
    model alone.
23. **A weight-log save still in flight does not take
    the meal screen.** If it finishes while the meal
    screen is up, the screen stays the meal screen,
    and the list row Esc will restore stays the row
    that was selected when the meal screen opened.
    The save still reloads the log. A form or month
    error that arrives while the meal screen is up
    stays on that form or sheet. Esc then shows the
    log, not the failed-load view that offers only
    `q quit`. A meal error is not cleared by that
    reload. It stays on the meal screen.

## Acceptance scenarios

Today in these scenarios is 10 November 1990 unless the
scenario freezes another day. The weight log, when it has
rows, has 1 November 1990 selected unless the scenario says
otherwise.

### S1 — `f` opens today, not the selected row

**Given** the daily list
**And** today is 10 November 1990
**And** the selected row is 1 November 1990
**When** `f` is pressed
**Then** the screen is the meal screen for 1990-11-10
**And** the heading contains `meals 1990-11-10`
**And** the day form is not shown
**And** the month sheet is not shown

### S2 — `f` opens today with no daily log

**Given** the daily list has no row for today
**And** today is 10 November 1990
**When** `f` is pressed
**Then** the meal screen shows 1990-11-10
**And** the daily list still has no row for that day
**And** no weight, sleep, steps, workout, or note was
written for that day

### S3 — Esc returns to the same log row

**Given** the meal screen was opened with the list on
1 November 1990
**When** Esc is pressed
**Then** the screen is the daily list
**And** 1 November 1990 is still selected
**And** the process does not quit

### S4 — `]` moves the meal date only

**Given** the list selection is 1 November 1990
**And** the meal screen shows 1990-11-10
**When** `]` is pressed
**Then** the meal screen shows 1990-11-11
**And** Esc returns to the list with 1 November 1990
still selected
**And** the month sheet opened from that list is
November 1990
**And** moving the meal date into December, then
returning, still opens November 1990

### S5 — `[` moves one calendar day

**Given** the meal screen shows 1992-03-01
**When** `[` is pressed
**Then** the meal screen shows 1992-02-29
**And** `]` returns it to 1992-03-01
**And** from 1990-01-01, `[` shows 1989-12-31

**And** the same row is selected

### S7 — `f` on the meal screen does not move

**Given** the meal screen shows 1990-11-11 after `]`
**When** `f` is pressed
**Then** the screen is still that meal screen
**And** the date is still 1990-11-11

line

## Functional requirements

-   **FR1.** `f` on the daily list opens the meal screen
    for today, even when another row is selected and even
    when today has no daily log row. It does not insert a
    daily log row. The heading contains `meals` and that
    date. The day form and the month sheet are not shown.
-   **FR2.** Esc from the idle meal screen returns to the
    list, leaves the selected row, and does not quit.
-   **FR3.** Idle `[` and `]` on the meal screen move one
    calendar day, including the 1992 leap day and the
    1989-12-31 boundary. They do not move the list
    selection or the month sheet. `[` and `]` on the
    daily list do nothing.
-   **FR4.** Idle `f` on the meal screen does not change
    the date or the lines. Idle `n`, `m`, `c`, `l`, `p`,
    `P`, and `t` do not leave the meal screen, do not
    change it, and do not write a PDF.
-   **FR5.** `f` on a month text cell still starts an
    edit whose text is `f`. `f` on the workout column
    does not. `f` on either chart does nothing and writes
    no PDF. `f` on a day-form text field is the letter
    `f`. `f` on the workout field does not toggle it.
    None of these open the meal screen.
-   **FR6.** Saving a meal does not change stored weight,
    sleep, steps, workout, note, or the trend text on the
    list. The list columns stay date, weight, trend,
    delta, sleep, steps, workout, and note. Neither chart
    shows the food name.
-   **FR7.** An empty date shows `calories 0.0`,
    `protein 0.0`, `carbohydrate 0.0`, and `fat 0.0`,
    with no selected line and no starter name. The screen
    does not contain `no allowance`. `x`, Enter, and `e`
    do nothing. `a` opens the add form.
-   **FR8.** `a` opens the add form on the current meal
    date, name first, marked with `>`. The heading keeps
    the date. Tab moves through the fields in Decision 15
    and wraps to the name. Tab writes nothing. A trimmed
    match asks only for grams and does not change the
    food. A new name asks for the four per-100 g numbers
    and then grams. Enter saves only when those fields
    match the trimmed name. A name changed since Tab is
    refused with `error:` and `name: press tab`, and
    nothing is written. After that Tab, a known name
    logs grams and does not write the four numbers. A
    new name inserts the food and the line together.
    Esc writes no line and no new food.
-   **FR9.** A blank name is refused with `error:` and
    `name: blank`. A name matches only after whitespace
    at either end is removed, and the match is
    case-sensitive. The stored name is the trimmed name.
    A middle space is part of the name.
-   **FR10.** A new food missing any of the four numbers,
    or given a non-number, is refused with `error:`, the
    field name, and `want a number`. A negative number is
    refused with `want a number that is not negative`.
    Zero is allowed. A nutrient uses the gram grammar,
    including on `e`: a leading `+` and one decimal
    point are allowed, and an exponent, `inf`, `NaN`,
    a slash, and a unit token are refused. A refused
    save writes no line and no new food. A missing
    number is not stored as zero. On `e`, a blank or a
    refused number refuses the whole edit.
-   **FR11.** Grams must match Decision 16. A fraction
    greater than zero is allowed. Zero, negative, a
    non-number, a slash fraction, an exponent, and a unit
    token are refused with `error:` and
    `grams: want a number greater than zero`. The form
    stays open. Stored grams are not replaced. A refused
    new food is not created. `100`, `+100`, and `.5` are
    100, 100, and 0.5 grams.
-   **FR12.** Totals are each per-100 g number times
    grams divided by 100, summed across the lines, shown
    to one decimal the way a body weight is shown.
    `1.25` grams paints as `1.2`. A total of 0.25
    paints as `0.2`. The idle gram weight uses that
    same one decimal. Exact
    zero is `0.0`, never `-0.0`. The line does not show
    its own four contributions. Stored grams are not
    replaced by that rounded text. Opening grams shows
    the stored value, and accepting it unchanged keeps
    the totals. `display_unit` pounds does not change
    the gram figures or the totals.
-   **FR13.** Up and down move the selection and stop at
    the ends. `j` and `k` do not move it. The first line
    is selected when a date with lines is shown. A saved
    line becomes the selection and is last. `x` removes
    the selected line immediately. The food remains. The
    next line is selected, or the new last line, or
    nothing if the date is empty. Empty totals are `0.0`.
-   **FR14.** Enter on the idle screen edits the selected
    line's grams. Enter accepts a value that passes
    FR11. Esc keeps the old grams. A refused value does
    not replace them. `e` edits the four numbers. Enter
    accepts. Esc keeps the old numbers. The name cannot
    be changed. Other lines and other dates that use the
    food follow a successful edit. Grams do not change.
    The food editor shows each stored number, and Enter
    on that text unchanged keeps it. A blank nutrient
    refuses the whole edit and does not store zero.
    There is no confirm.
-   **FR15.** While a form or an edit is open, `[` and
    `]` do not change the meal date. In the name field,
    `q` and `f` are letters and do not quit or open
    another screen. Idle `q` quits the same way as `q`
    on the list. `ctrl+c` quits from the meal screen,
    including from a field. Esc does not quit.
-   **FR16.** The help lines are the lines in
    Decision 20, including both list helps, the failed
    list, the idle meal screen, and the meal form.
-   **FR17.** After quit, the same database shows the
    same lines for that date, in the same order. Another
    date stays empty. An existing daily log row is
    unchanged. The daily log gains no meal column. The
    file stays mode `0600`. Meals do not create a
    database file and do not create a second one.
-   **FR18.** `docs/reference/keys.md` gains the rows in
    S41. `docs/how-to/meal-day.md` describes this screen
    only and names allowance, volume, recipes, and the
    starter set as later slices. `docs/how-to/README.md`
    lists that how-to. The `e` row and the how-to say
    that saving changes every meal date that uses the
    food. Neither asks for a confirm. The month, chart,
    and long-term bracket rows stay.
-   **FR19.** A weight-log save that finishes while the
    meal screen is up leaves that screen up and does
    not move the list row Esc will restore. The save
    still reloads the log. A day-form save and a
    month-cell save both obey this. A form or month
    error delivered on the meal screen does not turn
    the later list into the failed-load view. A meal
    error is still showing after a weight-log reload.

## Out of scope

-   A calorie allowance is a later slice, and this screen
    does not show remaining calories.
-   The words "no allowance" are not shown; that wording
    belongs to the allowance slice.
-   Ounces are not accepted as a food amount in this
    slice.
-   Pounds are not accepted as a food amount in this
    slice.
-   Cups are not accepted as a food amount in this slice.
-   Fluid ounces are not accepted as a food amount in
    this slice.
-   Tablespoons are not accepted as a food amount in
    this slice.
-   Teaspoons are not accepted as a food amount in this
    slice.
-   Milliliters are not accepted as a food amount in
    this slice.
-   A density in grams per milliliter is not stored and
    not asked for.
-   Recipes are not logged in this slice.
-   A fraction of a recipe is not logged in this slice.
-   The starter set of foods and recipes is not shipped.
-   Renaming a food is later work. This screen does
    not rename one.
-   Deleting a food is later work. Removing a line
    does not delete the food. The recipe slice's rule
    about an ingredient is not that operation.
-   A meal PDF is not written.
-   Blank log sheets are not part of this slice.
-   Long-term chart PDFs are not part of this slice.
-   Changing ApplyTrend is out of this slice.
-   Changing daily_log columns is out of this slice.
-   Turning on the alternate screen is out of this slice.
-   Turning on the mouse is out of this slice.
